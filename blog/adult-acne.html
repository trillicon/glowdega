Adulthood often brings expectations of clear, problem-free skin. Yet, for many, acne persists long after adolescence. If you're still battling breakouts despite having left your teenage years behind, you're not alone. Adult acne is a common and often frustrating condition that affects millions worldwide. This article aims to shed light on the causes, treatments, and management strategies for adult acne, with a focus on both conventional and holistic approaches.

Understanding the Underlying Causes

Before delving into solutions, it's crucial to understand why adult acne occurs. Several factors contribute to this persistent skin condition:

  1. Hormonal Fluctuations Hormonal changes don't end with puberty. Stress, menstrual cycles, pregnancy, workout regimen (or lack thereof), and hormonal disorders can all lead to adult acne breakouts. These fluctuations can increase oil production and inflammation in the skin.

  2. Chronic Stress The demands of adult life often come with increased stress levels. Stress triggers the release of cortisol, which can stimulate oil glands and promote inflammation, potentially exacerbating acne.

  3. Dietary Factors While the link between diet and acne is complex, some studies suggest that certain foods, particularly those high in refined sugars and dairy, may influence acne development in some individuals.

  4. Skincare and Cosmetic Products Ironically, the very products we use to care for our skin or enhance our appearance can sometimes contribute to acne. Using too many products or those that are comedogenic (pore-clogging) can lead to breakouts.

  5. Genetic Predisposition Genetics play a significant role in acne susceptibility. If your parents experienced adult acne, you may be more prone to it as well.

The RX ROute: Understanding Common Treatments

Many adults turn to prescription treatments for acne relief. While these can be effective, it's important to understand their mechanisms and potential limitations:

  1. Clindamycin is a topical antibiotic often prescribed to combat acne-causing bacteria. It works by penetrating the skin and hair follicles, where it inhibits the growth of Cutibacterium acnes (formerly known as Propionibacterium acnes), the primary bacteria associated with acne. By reducing the bacterial population, clindamycin helps decrease inflammation and prevent the formation of new acne lesions. Additionally, it may have a mild effect on reducing excess sebum production, further contributing to its acne-fighting properties. However, a 2020 study in the Journal of Clinical Medicine found that long-term use of topical antibiotics like Clindamycin can lead to antibiotic resistance. This resistance not only makes the treatment less effective over time but can also contribute to broader public health concerns related to antibiotic resistance.

  2. Tretinoin, a retinoid derived from vitamin A, is known for its ability to promote skin cell turnover. Its mechanism of action is multifaceted, targeting several key factors in acne development. Tretinoin works by binding to specific nuclear receptors in skin cells, which then alters gene expression related to cell growth and differentiation. This leads to increased cell turnover, effectively "unclogging" pores by promoting the shedding of dead skin cells and preventing their accumulation. Additionally, tretinoin has been shown to reduce sebum production and possess anti-inflammatory properties, further contributing to its efficacy in treating acne. While effective for many, a 2021 review in the American Journal of Clinical Dermatology highlighted that it often causes initial irritation, redness, and peeling. These side effects can be discouraging for some patients and may lead to discontinuation of the treatment before seeing benefits.

  3. Azelaic Acid is often touted as a gentler alternative to other topical treatments. While it is common to find azelaic acid without a prescription, the max percentage allowed over the counter in the United States is 10%. Prescription azelaic acid is typically found at a 15% or higher concentration. Its mechanism of action in treating acne is multifaceted and targets several key factors contributing to acne formation. Primarily, azelaic acid exhibits antimicrobial properties against Cutibacterium acnes, helping to reduce the bacterial population that plays a crucial role in acne development. Additionally, it acts as a mild exfoliant by inhibiting the production of keratin, a protein that can contribute to clogged pores when overproduced. Azelaic acid also possesses anti-inflammatory properties, helping to reduce redness and swelling associated with acne lesions. However, a 2022 study in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology found that while it can be effective for mild acne, its efficacy may be limited for moderate to severe cases. This suggests that it may not be a comprehensive solution for all adult acne sufferers.

  4. Spironolactone was originally designed to treat high blood pressure but has found a second life as an acne treatment due to its anti-androgenic properties. It works to treat acne primarily by its ability to block androgen receptors in the skin. By doing so, spironolactone reduces the effects of androgens (male hormones) on the sebaceous glands, leading to decreased sebum production. This is particularly beneficial for women with “hormonal acne”, as excess androgen activity is often a key factor in adult female acne. Additionally, spironolactone may have mild anti-inflammatory effects, further contributing to its acne-fighting properties. A 2023 study in JAMA Dermatology revealed that while effective, it can take up to six months to see significant results. Moreover, it's not suitable for everyone, particularly men, due to its hormonal effects.

  5. Oral Contraceptives can sometimes be FDA-approved for acne treatment. While the very nature of the drug is designed to regulate hormones, some are better at reducing the levels of circulating androgens. Oral contraceptives containing both estrogen and progestin work by suppressing ovarian androgen production and increasing sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) levels. SHBG binds to free testosterone in the bloodstream, reducing its availability to stimulate sebaceous gland activity. Additionally, some progestins used in oral contraceptives have anti-androgenic properties, further contributing to their acne-fighting effects by directly blocking androgen receptors in the skin.A 2022 review in the International Journal of Women's Dermatology noted their effectiveness but also highlighted potential side effects, including mood changes and an increased risk of blood clots. This underscores the importance of careful consideration and medical supervision when using hormonal treatments for acne.

While these treatments can be effective for some individuals, they are not universal solutions. Each comes with its own set of potential side effects and limitations, emphasizing the need for personalized treatment approaches.

A Holistic Approach: Treating Skin Health Comprehensively

Given the complex nature of adult acne and the limitations of some prescription treatments, a holistic approach to skin health can be beneficial. This strategy addresses not just the symptoms but the underlying causes of acne:

  1. Professional Skin Analysis A comprehensive skin health examination can uncover the root causes of breakouts. This goes beyond surface-level observations to consider factors like skin type, sensitivity, and underlying health conditions that may contribute to acne. At Glowdega, we start each new client treatment with a diagnostic skin health exam. And if you have concerns that go beyond that, we have a wonderful network of health practitioners we can refer out for bloodwork or more in-depth medical analysis.

  2. Customized Treatment Plans One-size-fits-all solutions rarely work for adult acne. Customized treatment plans take into account individual skin types, lifestyles, and specific acne triggers to create a more effective and sustainable approach to clear skin. We provide each client with both customized routines and treatment plans to ensure you are using products and getting treatments optimized for your concerns.

  3. Advanced Professional Treatments Professional treatments such as chemical peels, microchanneling, or advanced exfoliation techniques can provide more intensive care for stubborn acne. These treatments, when performed by skilled professionals like us, can address issues that over-the-counter or prescription topicals may not effectively target.

  4. Lifestyle Modifications Addressing lifestyle factors such as stress management, sleep quality, and diet can have a significant impact on skin health. These changes, while sometimes challenging to implement, can lead to long-term improvements in both skin condition and overall well-being.

Prevention: The Key to Long-Term Skin Health

While treating existing acne is important, preventing new breakouts is equally crucial. Here are some evidence-based prevention strategies:

  • Consistent Cleansing: Wash your face twice daily to remove excess oil and debris without over-stripping the skin. We typically advise our clients to use a gentle cleanser in the mornings and a treatment cleanser in the evenings. And we always advise that clients wipe away their cleansers with a warm washcloth.

  • Hydration Help: Even oily skin needs hydration. We can’t stress how important it is to use a hydrating toner (like Hydra Cadabra) in your routine along with a hydrating serum (Barrier Brew and P2 are great hydrators to use too). And of course you want to seal this all in with a lightweight, non-comedogenic moisturizer to maintain skin barrier health.

  • Prioritize Patch Testing: Before incorporating a new product into your routine, patch test it for at least 24 hours to check for any adverse reactions. You can patch test on your inner elbow or just below your ear on your neck. Be on the lookout for itchiness, redness, stinging, or any other sign of irritation.

Your Personalized Path to Clear Skin

Dealing with adult acne can be frustrating, but it's important to remember that clear, healthy skin is achievable with the right approach. A comprehensive, personalized strategy that addresses the multiple factors contributing to acne is often the most effective path forward.

That’s what we do at Glowdega. Our focus is on providing this kind of holistic, individualized care to all of our clients no matter their concerns. By combining professional analysis, customized treatment plans, and lifestyle guidance, these approaches aim to not just clear your acne but to promote overall skin health.
